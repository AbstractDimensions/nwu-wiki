## What the module covers

- **Orthographic projection** — first- and third-angle, the core of everything
- **Isometric drawing** — freehand and instrument work
- **Sectional views** — full, half and offset sections
- **Dimensioning and tolerances** — the part students lose the most marks on
- **AutoCAD basics** — second half of the semester, lab-based

### The drawing kit

You need your own: A3 drawing board, T-square or drafting machine access,
set squares (45° + 30/60°), compass set, clutch pencil (0.5mm) and eraser
shield. Buy once in week 1 — the queue at the campus shop in week 3 is brutal.

## Assessment breakdown

> [!warning]
> Illustrative weights — confirm against the current study guide before
> planning anything.

| Item | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly practicals | 40% | Best 8 of 10; check the study guide for absence rules |
| Class tests (×2) | 20% | Drawing-based, bring your full kit |
| Exam | 40% | 3 hours, 50% subminimum |

> [!warning]
> Practical hand-in is at the **start** of the session, not the end. Walking in
> ten minutes late with an unfinished sheet is a zero.

## How to pass

1. Do every practical the night it's issued, not the night before hand-in.
2. Redraw your worst sheet each week — the exam repeats practical tasks.
3. Learn the dimensioning rules by heart; they're free marks or free losses.
