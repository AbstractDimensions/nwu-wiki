# Bursaries Guide

**The short version:** bursaries in South Africa are applied for *before* the
academic year starts, they're competitive, and the ones nobody applies to are
your best odds. Start in **February–May** for the following year.

> [!warning]
> Never pay anyone to "apply for you" or to "guarantee" a bursary. Legitimate
> bursaries never charge an application fee. If someone asks for money for a
> "guaranteed placement", it is a scam — report it to the institution named.

## The timeline that matters

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Feb–Apr** | Big corporate and government bursaries open for the next year |
| **Apr–Jun** | ISFAP/NSFAS-type funding applications run (they close early — treat deadlines as *before* the published date) |
| **Jun–Aug** | Most company bursary deadlines |
| **Aug–Oct** | Second-round / leftover funding, university merit awards |
| **Nov–Jan** | Late private-sector bursaries; confirm NSFAS/ISFAP status |

Closing dates shift every year. The list in the
[Bursary Directory](#/bursary-directory) tells you *who* funds; the company's
own careers page tells you *when*.

## What a bursary usually covers — read the fine print

A "full bursary" often means: **tuition + a book/meal allowance, sometimes
accommodation**. Common strings attached:

- **Work-back / vacation work** — many corporate bursaries require you to work
  for them during vacations, and to join them after graduating for as many
  years as they funded you. Check whether this is *optional* or *binding*.
- **Minimum marks** — typically "pass all modules", sometimes with a specific
  average per year. Failing can mean repaying.
- **Registration only vs. everything** — some cover only tuition, leaving you to
  fund accommodation and food.
- **Recipient-funded modules** — a few exclude certain degrees or only fund
  specific fields (usually engineering, health sciences, IT, teaching).

## The documents you'll need (prepare once, reuse all year)

- Certified copy of your ID
- Final school results / latest academic transcript
- Proof of registration or acceptance letter
- Proof of income (guardian/parents) — payslips or an affidavit if unemployed
- A short motivation letter and CV

> [!tip]
> Write one good CV and one strong motivation paragraph early in the year, then
> tailor 20%. Students who rewrite from scratch for each application apply to
> three; students who reuse apply to twenty.

## Where to actually find them

- The **[Bursary Directory](#/bursary-directory)** on this wiki — a large
  student-maintained list of organisations that fund students.
- **Careers portals** of companies near you — most list a "Bursaries" page.
- **NSFAS** (nsfas.org.za) for government needs-based funding.
- **ISFAP** (isfap.co.za) for the "missing middle" — students who don't qualify
  for NSFAS but can't afford full fees.
- Your faculty office — some small merit awards are never advertised.

## Applying well

1. Apply to **more than you think you need** — ten applications is normal.
2. Meet every listed requirement exactly; incomplete applications are binned
   first at scale.
3. Keep a spreadsheet: organisation, what it covers, deadline, status, login.
4. Follow up by email two weeks after the deadline — it occasionally matters.
